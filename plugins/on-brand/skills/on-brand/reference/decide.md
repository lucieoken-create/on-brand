# Decide: the questions only the person can answer

Extraction shows how the brand looks today. It can't tell you what each color is for, what the brand would never do, or why. Those answers are what keep AI-made work from drifting back to generic, so this step matters most.

## How to ask

- **One question at a time.** Wait for the answer before the next one.
- **Lead with a suggestion** drawn from what `pull` found, so they react instead of starting cold: "Your buttons and links use the coral. Should coral be the one accent, used only for things people click?"
- **Plain words.** Say "the color for headlines", not "primary token". Say "how rounded the corners are", not "border radius".
- **Accept short answers.** "Yes", "not that", "whatever you think" are fine. For "whatever you think", make the call, say what you chose and why, and move on.
- **Ten questions at most.** Skip any question the pull already answered with confidence; just confirm it in the summary.
- If there was nothing to pull (no site, no guide), ask the same questions with examples instead of suggestions, and offer two or three concrete options for colors and fonts.

## The questions

Ask in this order. The wording is a guide; adjust it to the person and what you found.

1. **Point of view.** "Who are you making things for, and what should they feel in the first three seconds of seeing one of your posts?"
2. **Colors and their jobs.** Confirm the colors found, then: "Which one should do most of the work, which is the background, and which is the one accent that only shows up for important things?" Get a rough share for each, like mostly warm white, navy for text, coral rarely.
3. **Type.** "One font for headlines and one for everything else. Here's what your site uses. Keep them, or is there a reason they don't feel right?" Note why the fonts fit, in their words.
4. **Shape, space and motion.** "Square corners or rounded? Lots of breathing room, or packed with information? Anything that moves?"
5. **Refusals.** "What would never appear in your brand? Colors, styles, words, kinds of photos, anything." Push gently for specifics: "Anything you've seen a competitor do that you'd never do?" This is the most important question. Aim for at least five items.
6. **Assets.** "Do you have a logo file, photos you own, illustrations or patterns we should use? Anything we should never use, like an old logo?"
7. **Reasoning.** "When something you've made looks off, what's usually the reason?" (Too busy, wrong color, looks cheap, looks like everyone else.) The answer becomes a rule.
8. **Where it shows up.** "What do you make most often: social posts or banners, email newsletters, slides, PDFs like proposals or guides, flyers, web pages?" This decides which format files get written, and which platforms to note in the social file. Default to social posts and documents if they're unsure.
9. **Words on the page.** "How much text should go on an image? For example, a headline of six words or fewer and no more than four or five short phrases. What goes on the graphic, and what goes in the caption? Sentence case or title case for headlines?"
10. **Copy habits.** "Any words, punctuation or habits that should never show up? Exclamation points, emoji, ALL CAPS, certain buzzwords? How is your business name written? How do you write prices, dates and calls to action?"

Voice gets one line in PRODUCT.md (three words for how the brand sounds). If they want more than that, suggest a separate voice skill.

## The summary

Before writing anything, show one screen that covers every decision, grouped: who it's for, colors with jobs and shares, type, shape and space, never do, assets, where it shows up, copy rules. Mark anything still estimated. Mark any contrast pair that fails.

End with: "Anything to change before I write your files?" Only move on to `reference/write.md` after a yes or their changes.
