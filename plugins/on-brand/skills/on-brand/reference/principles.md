# What to check before anything goes out

These are Lucie Oken's working rules for keeping AI-made pieces from looking like everyone else's. Read them before making any piece and again before showing it. They are about sameness, the habits that make work look machine-made. They are not a style.

**The person's own system wins.** If DESIGN.md or PRODUCT.md says otherwise (a brand that is loud and packed on purpose, a brand that centers everything by choice), follow the system and skip the rule that conflicts.

1. **One thing leads.** Every piece has one element that is clearly the biggest or boldest. When the headline, the date and the button all compete, nothing gets read.
2. **The accent stays rare.** One accent color, on the one thing that matters: the date, the price, the button. An accent used everywhere stops being an accent.
3. **The headline says something.** A specific fact or claim in the brand's own words. If the line could sit on a competitor's post unchanged, rewrite it.
4. **Cut words before shrinking type.** If the copy doesn't fit, the copy is too long. Small type on an image is the first thing people skip.
5. **Labels are plain, and there's one of them.** The small line above a headline is a structural word or a date. It never carries the point, and two stacked labels is one too many.
6. **No boxes inside boxes.** A card in a card, or a border plus a shadow plus a tinted background on the same shape, is decoration piling up. Pick one way to separate things.
7. **Decoration needs a job.** Gradient blobs, sparkles, an icon on every bullet and emoji in headings are what AI reaches for when it has nothing to say. If removing it loses nothing, remove it.
8. **Alignment is a choice, and it gets checked.** This is the most common problem in a first pass. Everything centered is the default, and it's why so much AI work looks the same. Decide where things line up, then line everything up to it:
   - Text blocks share one left edge (or one center line). No block sits a few pixels off the others.
   - Items in a row share a top edge and a height. Columns are the same width unless there's a reason.
   - The space between things comes from the spacing scale and repeats: the same gap between every card, the same gap under every heading.
   - Text inside a box is placed the same way in every box: all top-aligned or all centered.
   - Before showing the piece, look at it and trace the edges. Fix anything that is nearly aligned, because nearly is what reads as sloppy.
9. **The signature element is used sparingly.** A brand's pattern, illustration or motif is strongest when it appears once in a set. On every piece it becomes wallpaper.
10. **Images are placed on purpose.** A photo or figure sits fully inside the frame with room around it, or runs off an edge deliberately. Faces get breathing room. Use real photos the brand owns, or none.
11. **Leave room at the edges.** Nothing touches the edge of the canvas, and the margin is the same on every piece in a set.
12. **Look at it finished, at the size it will be seen.** Shrink a post to phone size before calling it done. Check every page of a PDF. Most problems only show up there.
13. **Type has a fixed hierarchy.** Pick a few sizes and weights and give each one a job: one for the thing that leads, one for headings, one for reading, one for labels. Use nothing else. Five sizes where two would do, or a heavier weight with no reason behind it, is the quickest way a piece starts to look machine-made.
14. **Precise to the pixel.** Use whole-pixel sizes and positions, with no fractions. Check the finished piece at full size and zoomed in: edges that are half a pixel off, text that renders blurry, a rule that's 1px in one place and 2px in another. Nobody notices when it's right; everyone feels it when it's wrong.

## Using these

- **When making:** build with them in mind, then run down the list before showing the piece. Fix what fails without being asked.
- **When checking someone's piece:** name which rule a problem falls under, in plain words, and say what to change.
- **When the person pushes back on one:** it's their brand. Offer to write their preference into their system (DESIGN.md or PRODUCT.md), so it holds next time.
