# Print: flyers, one-pagers and posters

Starter rules. Fill in the brand-specific lines when writing a system.

## Sizes

| Piece | Size | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flyer or one-pager (US) | Letter, 8.5 × 11 in | Default in the US. |
| Flyer or one-pager (elsewhere) | A4, 210 × 297 mm | |
| Half-page flyer | 5.5 × 8.5 in | Good for counters and bags. |
| Small poster | 11 × 17 in (tabloid) | For shop windows. |

- Keep all text at least 0.5 in (12 mm) from the trimmed edge.
- If color runs to the edge, add 0.125 in (3 mm) bleed on every side and tell the person to choose "print with bleed" at the print shop. A home printer can't print to the edge, so keep a white margin for anything printed at home.

## Type sizes

- Headline: {{headline font}} at 48 to 96pt on a letter flyer. It should read from across a room.
- Body: {{body font}} at 10 to 12pt. Never below 9pt.
- Fine print: 8pt at the smallest.

## Words

A flyer is read in passing. Headline, the one fact that matters most (date, offer), where and when, and how to find out more. {{Their word limits.}} Anything longer belongs on a one-pager, which can hold a short intro, three to five points, and contact details.

## Color

Print colors look darker and duller than on screen. Avoid very pale text on white and very dark text on dark colors. Big areas of a dark color use a lot of ink on home printers; for anything printed at home, keep large fills light.

## Images and codes

- Photos at 300 dpi at their printed size. A phone photo is usually fine at half-page size; a web image usually isn't.
- QR codes at least 0.8 in (2 cm) square, dark on light, with a few words saying where they go.

## Making the file

Build as HTML with `@page` set to the size, then print to PDF. Check that fonts are embedded (open the PDF and look at the headline) and that nothing sits closer than the margin to the edge.
